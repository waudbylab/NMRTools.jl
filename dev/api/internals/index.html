The real spectrum: the purely real component of every point of a multicomplex spectrum, real in every dimension, as an NMRData with Float64 elements. This is the final step of a processing pipeline.

NMRTools.NMRBase._phasetraces — Method
_phasetraces(spec, di; maxtraces=64, factor=8) -> (traces, exclude)

1D traces along array dimension di from which its phase is estimated, and chemical-shift intervals along di to leave out of them.

When another dimension of spec is a frequency dimension, the traces pass through the strongest cross peaks. Peaks are local maxima of the magnitude (the largest over the components) that stand out by more than factor times the noise of their own point along di, measured as the median absolute deviation over the other dimensions. Scaling by this local noise suppresses ridges along the other dimensions, such as t₁ noise from a residual solvent signal. Points along di whose median magnitude over the other dimensions is far above the typical one are such ridges, and are returned in exclude. At most maxtraces traces are used, one per position in the other dimensions.

The components of a trace that are real and imaginary in the other dimensions' imaginary units all share the phase of its lines along di, differing only by real factors that depend on how the other dimensions are phased. Each trace is the real combination of these components with the most signal (the leading eigenvector of their real Gram matrix, with a non-negative weight on the component real in every other unit), so it does not depend on the phases of the other dimensions. Traces through the peaks also keep apart lines that would overlap in a projection.

Otherwise (for 1D data and for series such as pseudo-2D experiments), or when no cross peak is found, the spectrum itself is returned, and its phase is estimated from _referencetrace.

NMRTools.NMRBase._referencetrace — Method
_referencetrace(spec, di) -> Vector{ComplexF64}

Complex trace along array dimension di of spec, in the imaginary unit given by that dimension's :mcindex. Other imaginary units take their real component. Other dimensions are reduced so that every signal contributes: a time dimension contributes its first point, a frequency dimension is summed over the points that carry signal (see _signalindices), and any other dimension is summed.

A frequency dimension is not summed over all its points: that sum is its first time point, which is dominated by the solvent and baseline rather than the cross peaks, and it adds the noise of every point while signal comes from only a few.

NMRTools.NMRBase._reflectroots — Method
_reflectroots(a, direction) -> Vector{Complex}

Stabilise LP coefficients a by reflecting the roots of the prediction polynomial across the unit circle: for :forward extension, roots with |z|>1 (growing) are mapped to 1/conj(z) (inside); for :backward, roots with |z|<1 are pushed outside. Coefficients are rebuilt from the reflected roots.

NMRTools.NMRBase._removegroupdelay! — Method
_removegroupdelay!(A, unit, di, g)

Remove a digital-filter group delay of g points from the spectrum A (immediately after the FFT along array dimension di, before fftshift) by multiplying by the linear phase ramp exp(2πi·g·k/N), with k the signed frequency index. This is the Fourier shift theorem: it moves the time origin forward by g points, so build-up points stored before t = 0 end up at negative time, as in TopSpin and NMRPipe.

NMRTools.NMRBase._zhubaxforward — Method
_zhubaxforward(x, order, rootfix) -> Vector{Complex}

Forward-extension LP coefficients by the Zhu–Bax scheme: fit backward LP, treat the roots of its prediction polynomial (Σⱼ bⱼwʲ = 1) as the candidate poles, reflect any with |z|>1 to 1/conj(z) so the forward extrapolation cannot grow, and rebuild forward coefficients. This separates signal poles (inside the unit circle) from noise poles more cleanly than a direct forward fit, and is the variant NMR usually prefers. With rootfix=false, falls back to a plain forward least-squares fit.

NMRTools.NMRIO._groupdelay — Function
_groupdelay(acqusdic) -> Float64

The Bruker digital-filter group delay (points) of the direct dimension: the number of points of filter build-up ("lead-in") that precede time zero in the stored FID. The data are not modified; the lead-in stays in the FID at negative times and ft removes the delay with a linear phase ramp after the Fourier transform. Which value applies is set by the acquisition parameters:

  • DIGMOD = 0 (analogue filtering): no group delay.
  • DSPFVS 20–23: GRPDLY. For baseopt acquisitions (DIGMOD = 3) it is an integer and the spectrometer has already corrected the initial points of the FID.
  • DSPFVS 10–19 (older data): the delay should be looked up from DECIM and DSPFVS. This is not implemented; GRPDLY is used if valid, otherwise no delay is applied, with a warning.
  • DSPFVS outside 10–23: not a firmware version Bruker defines. TopSpin's cryoproc1d, for example, sets DSPFVS = 0 after rewriting the FID with its start removed: the filter build-up and some points after time zero. The number of points removed is found by comparing TD in acqu (the original) and acqus; the points beyond GRPDLY were after time zero, so the stored FID starts that many dwells after t = 0. This is returned as a negative delay, so that ft restores the time origin. A warning reports the missing points. If acqu does not show how many were removed, no delay is applied.

NMRTools.NMRIO.resolve_programmatic_lists! — Method
resolve_programmatic_lists!(annotations::Dict{String, Any}, spec::NMRData)

Resolve programmatic list patterns in annotations to actual vectors.

Programmatic lists are specified as dictionaries with one of two patterns:

Pattern 1: Type-based (linear/log)

  • type: "linear" or "log" (logarithmic)
  • start: starting value
  • step: step size (for linear with start/step)
  • end: ending value (for linear or log with start/end)

Pattern 2: Counter-based

  • counter: array of values (typically integers from a loop counter)
  • scale: scale factor to multiply each counter element by

For type-based patterns, the function:

  1. Identifies fields containing programmatic list patterns ({type, start, step/end})
  2. Finds the dimension number for that field from the dimensions annotation
  3. Gets the number of points in that dimension
  4. Replaces the pattern with a correctly sized vector

For counter-based patterns, the function:

  1. Multiplies each element of the counter array by the scale value
  2. Returns the resulting vector

Examples

# Linear spacing with start/step
# {type: linear, start: p9, step: p9} with 10 points → [p9, 2*p9, 3*p9, ...]

# Linear spacing with start/end
# {type: linear, start: 0.001, end: 0.1} with 10 points → [0.001, 0.012, ..., 0.1]

# Logarithmic spacing with start/end
# {type: log, start: 0.001, end: 0.1} with 10 points → [0.001, 0.0016, ..., 0.1]

# Counter-based pattern
# {counter: [0, 1, 2, 3], scale: 0.01} → [0.0, 0.01, 0.02, 0.03]
